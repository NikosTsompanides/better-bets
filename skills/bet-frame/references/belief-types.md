# Sorting beliefs

Four buckets. The sort matters more than the taxonomy — the point is that
assumptions stop being invisible.

**Fact.** You can name the evidence. "The p99 is 840ms" is a fact if someone
read it off a dashboard this week. It is an assumption if someone remembers it
being roughly that.

**Assumption.** Believed, not established. Most decisions that go wrong go
wrong here, and the dangerous assumptions are the ones nobody noticed making.
"The team can absorb this alongside current work." "Customers want this."
"Migrating later will be about as hard as migrating now."

**Inference.** Derived. Only as sound as the derivation and the inputs. "Churn
rose after the pricing change, so the pricing change caused churn" is an
inference with a competing explanation sitting right next to it.

**Opinion.** Judgment where evidence is thin. Perfectly legitimate — experience
is real. The failure is opinion presented as fact, especially confident opinion
from a senior voice or from an AI.

## Edge cases worth knowing

**Facts with an expiry date.** "We have 40 enterprise customers" was a fact in
March. Treat anything time-sensitive as an assumption unless it was checked
recently.

**Facts about other people's intentions.** "Legal will approve this" is never a
fact until legal has said so. Same for what a vendor will support, what a
competitor will do, what a colleague will agree to.

**Borrowed facts.** Something true of another company, another codebase,
another market is an analogy, not evidence about yours.

**Facts the AI supplied.** If you produced it from training rather than from
checking, it is an assumption. Say so. This is the single most common way a
false fact enters a decision that involves an agent.

## The test

For each statement, ask: *what would I have to do to check this, and have I
done it?* If the answer to the second half is no, it is not a fact.
