# Reframing worked examples

The pattern: a question naming a solution becomes a question naming the
outcome, with the alternatives back in view.

**Engineering.** "Should we use Kafka?" → "What is the best way to handle our
expected event volume without adding operational load we cannot carry?" Kafka
is now one candidate among managed queues, a database-backed outbox, and doing
nothing yet.

**Hiring.** "Should we hire a senior backend engineer?" → "What is the
cheapest way to clear the backend bottleneck within a quarter?" Now competing
with contracting, reprioritising, or removing the work.

**Product.** "Should we build a mobile app?" → "What is the best way to serve
the 30% of usage that happens away from a desk?" A responsive web app is back
on the table.

**Strategy.** "Should we move upmarket?" → "Where does the next £2m of revenue
come from at acceptable cost of sale?" Upmarket is one answer; expansion
revenue and a cheaper channel are others.

**Personal.** "Should I take the offer?" → "What do I most want the next two
years to look like, and which available option gets closest?" The offer is one
option; renegotiating and staying are others.

## Vagueness is a different problem

"Should we improve onboarding?" is not solution-shaped, it is unanswerable —
nothing would settle it. Ask what would count as success and by when: "Can we
get first-week activation from 22% to 35% by end of quarter without adding
headcount?" is a bet you can actually resolve.

## When not to reframe

If the user has already done this work, reframing again is patronising and
wastes a turn. If they push back and say the original framing is right, take
it — they have context you do not. Reframe once, explain why, move on.
