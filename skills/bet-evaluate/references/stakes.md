# Stakes checklist

Walk it, name only what applies. A stakes list padded with non-applicable rows
trains the reader to skim.

- **Downside** — what goes wrong, and its *shape*. A small expected loss with a
  catastrophic tail is a different decision from a steady moderate loss.
- **Upside** — what you are actually playing for. Decisions get evaluated on
  risk alone and quietly become risk-avoidance exercises.
- **Blast radius** — who is affected beyond the decider. One team, every
  customer, the public.
- **Opportunity cost** — what does not happen because this does. Usually the
  largest cost and the least often written down.
- **Money** — direct spend, and the run-rate it commits you to.
- **Time** — elapsed, not just effort. Six weeks of one person is a quarter of
  their year.
- **Reputation** — internal credibility and external perception. Slow to
  rebuild.
- **Customers** — trust, workflow disruption, migration burden you impose.
- **Safety, security, privacy** — where relevant, these dominate everything
  else and should be assessed first, not last.
- **Cost of delay** — the price of not deciding yet. Sometimes zero. Sometimes
  the largest number on the list. Always ask.

## Asymmetry

Two questions that do more work than the full list:

*What is the worst realistic outcome, and can we survive it?* Survivable
downside plus real upside is a bet worth making even at low confidence.
Unsurvivable downside is not, at any confidence.

*Is the downside bounded?* Bounded losses can be sized and accepted. Unbounded
ones — reputational, legal, safety — cannot be traded off against expected
value in the same way.
