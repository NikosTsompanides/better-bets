# Calibration

## The outside view

Before reasoning from the specifics, ask what usually happens in cases like
this one. Then adjust from there.

The inside view builds an estimate from the details: this team, this codebase,
this quarter, these mitigations. It feels rigorous and it is reliably
optimistic, because the details you can see are the ones you have planned for
and the failures come from the ones you cannot.

Useful base rates, when you have nothing better: migrations overrun. Senior
hires take longer to source than planned. Features are used less than
predicted. Integrations with third parties take longer than their docs suggest.
Estimates made without a comparable completed project are worth little.

If your estimate departs sharply from the base rate, say what justifies the
departure. "This time is different" is sometimes true and always the thing
everyone says.

## The wager test

Ask what you would actually bet, at even odds, on the belief being right.

"Pretty confident" survives until the stake is real. Someone who will not put
£500 on their 80% is not at 80%. Run it on your own statements too — if you
would not wager on a claim you just made, downgrade how you stated it.

The point is not to bet. The point is that imagining a stake makes vague
confidence concrete, and vague confidence is where overconfidence hides.

## Expressing confidence

Prefer basis to number:

- *"Low — this rests on one customer conversation."*
- *"Moderate — we have run three similar migrations, all landed within 20% of
  estimate."*
- *"High — measured directly last week."*

A number is fine when something supports it: a base rate over enough cases, a
measurement, a genuine track record. State what supports it in the same breath.
A bare percentage borrows the authority of measurement without doing the work.

## Confidence is not a summary of how good the option is

Confidence is about the *belief*, not the option. You can be highly confident
that a mediocre option is mediocre. Conflating the two produces confident
recommendations for things nobody has checked.
