# Product prioritisation: what goes in the quarter

Shows the framework where the decision is a ranking, not a yes/no.

## bet-frame

**Asked:** "Should we build the reporting dashboard or the integrations?"

**Reframed:** *Which single quarter of work most reduces the reason customers
leave us in year two?* Both candidates stay, but now they are competing on
something measurable rather than on advocacy.

**Facts.** Year-two churn is 31%. Nine of the last twenty churn interviews
mentioned reporting. Four mentioned a missing integration, three of those the
same one.

**Assumptions.** Churn interviews represent silent churners — unverified and
probably false, since people who take the exit call are unusually engaged.
Reporting mentions describe the same underlying need. Building either changes
behaviour rather than just satisfying a stated preference.

**Unknown that would change the answer.** Whether reporting is the reason
people leave or the thing they name because it is easiest to articulate.

## bet-evaluate

**Confidence.** Low that either fixes churn. The evidence is stated preference
from a biased sample.

**Stakes.** A quarter of the team either way. The real cost is opportunity:
the thing not built. Cost of delay is one quarter of continued churn.

**Reversibility.** Easy in principle, hard in practice — shipped features
acquire users, and removing one costs more than never building it.

**Alternatives.** Reporting. Integrations. Higher-upside challenger: neither —
spend three weeks finding out why *silent* churners actually leave, then commit
the remaining ten weeks with better information.

**Size.** Low confidence, moderate downside, harder to reverse than it looks →
the challenger is the right shape.

## bet-experiment

**Deciding uncertainty:** whether reporting is cause or vocabulary.

**Probe:** fake-door a reporting entry point for two weeks and count who clicks
through and what they try to do; in parallel, five interviews with accounts
that lapsed without an exit call.

**Criteria.** Above 15% of active accounts engaging, with consistent intent →
build reporting. Diffuse or low engagement → the mentions were vocabulary, and
the churn cause is elsewhere.

## bet-update

**Committed** after the probe: 6% engaged, and the five lapsed accounts
described onboarding failure, not reporting. Neither original candidate was
built. The quarter went to first-run experience.

**Superseded in place:** ~~reporting is the leading churn driver~~ — superseded
2026-04-02, the fake door did not support it.

## bet-review

Year-two churn moved 31% → 24%. **Good decision, good outcome**, but the review
caught something: the win came from the three-week probe, not from the ten
weeks of building. The belief worth carrying is about the *sequencing*, not
about onboarding.

**Belief updated:** exit-interview themes describe what customers can articulate,
not what drives them. Weight them accordingly.
